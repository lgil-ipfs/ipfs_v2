Three needs every protection plan should cover

Insurance is really about one thing: making sure the plans you've made still happen if something unexpected happens to you. Most families have three distinct needs.

Disability insurance

The need: replacing your income if an illness or injury keeps you from working.

Your paycheque pays the mortgage, groceries, and savings. Disability coverage keeps a portion of it coming while you recover.

Critical illness insurance

The need: protecting your savings through a prolonged absence from work due to a serious illness.

A tax-free lump sum after a diagnosis like cancer, a heart attack, or a stroke, so treatment, travel, and time off don't drain your RRSP or emergency fund.

Life insurance

The need: protecting your family's standard of living if you pass away.

It can pay off the mortgage and debts, replace your income for the years your family depends on it, and cover taxes so family property stays in the family.

Why this matters: the cottage tax bill

Picture a family cottage bought years ago for $200,000 that's now worth $1,000,000. When the owner passes away, the Canada Revenue Agency treats the cottage as if it were sold at today's value. Half of the $800,000 gain is taxable, which can mean a tax bill of roughly $200,000 on the final tax return.

That bill has to be paid before the estate can be settled. With no insurance or cash set aside, the family often has only one option: sell the cottage to pay the tax. The place where summers were spent leaves the family for good. A life insurance policy sized to the tax bill lets the next generation keep it.

A surviving spouse can usually inherit without tax, which delays the bill until the second death.
