How Much Life Insurance Do You Actually Need?

Rules of thumb like "ten times your salary" are easy, but they're rarely right. Here's how to work out a number that fits your family.

By Iberian Pacific Financial Services October 22, 2026 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Start with your biggest obligations: the mortgage and other debts.
  • Add the income your family would need, and for how many years.
  • Include final expenses, education goals, and any taxes on family property.
  • Subtract savings and the coverage you already have, including through work.

Life insurance has one job: making sure the plans you've made for your family still happen if you're not there. The right amount depends on what those plans cost, which is why a quick multiple of your salary is rarely accurate.

Start with the biggest obligations

For most families, the largest need is the mortgage. Paying it off means no one has to sell the home or take on the payments alone. Add any other debts, like a car loan or line of credit, plus final expenses such as funeral and estate costs.

Then replace your income

Once the debts are handled, your family will still need money to live on. Ask two questions: what share of your income would they need each year, and for how long? With no mortgage payment, many families need 50% to 70% of the income they've lost, often until the youngest child is independent.

Add your goals

Education for your children, a gift to a charity, or the tax bill on a family cottage are all needs life insurance can cover. Taxes on property are easy to overlook and can be large. We explain why in Keeping the Family Cottage.

Then subtract what you already have

Savings and investments your family could use reduce the need, and so does coverage you already have. Be careful with group life insurance through work: it's often only one or two times your salary, and it usually ends if you change jobs.

Example

A family has a $500,000 mortgage, $30,000 of other debt, and $25,000 for final expenses. They'd need $60,000 a year for 15 years, which takes roughly $770,000 set aside today, invested to earn 2% above inflation. That's about $1.3 million in total. With $50,000 of savings and $250,000 of group coverage, the remaining need is about $1 million.

Term or permanent?

Term insurance covers you for a set period, like 20 years, and costs less, which makes it a good fit for needs that end, like a mortgage or raising children. Permanent insurance lasts for life and suits needs that never go away, such as estate taxes or a legacy gift. Many families use a mix of both.

Don't forget disability and critical illness

You're more likely to miss work because of an illness or injury than to pass away during your working years. Disability insurance replaces part of your income, and critical illness insurance pays a lump sum so a long recovery doesn't drain your savings.
