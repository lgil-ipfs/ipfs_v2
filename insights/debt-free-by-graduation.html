A Plan to Be Debt Free by Graduation

Iberian Pacific started with a friend who was struggling with his student loans. Here's the plan we built together, and how any student can use it.

By Iberian Pacific Financial Services October 22, 2026 6 min read

Key takeaways

  • Know exactly what you owe, including which loans charge interest.
  • A simple monthly budget is the foundation of any payoff plan.
  • Put summer, co-op, and part-time income to work as lump-sum payments.
  • Keep a small emergency fund so a surprise expense doesn't become new debt.

Iberian Pacific began with a conversation between friends. A close friend of our founder was falling behind on his student loans and didn't know where to start. Instead of handing him generic advice, we sat down together, mapped out every dollar coming in and going out, and built a plan to make him 100% debt free by graduation. It worked, and it shaped everything we've done since.

The plan wasn't complicated. It came down to five steps that any student can follow.

1. Know exactly what you owe

Start with a list of every debt: the lender, the balance, the interest rate, and the minimum payment. Students are often surprised by what they find.

  • Federal student loans: since April 1, 2023, the federal portion of Canada Student Loans no longer accrues interest.
  • Provincial student loans: rules vary by province. Some, like British Columbia, are interest-free, while others charge interest.
  • Student lines of credit and credit cards: these almost always charge interest, and credit cards charge a lot of it.

This list tells you where your money does the most good. A dollar paid toward a credit card charging 20% saves far more than a dollar paid toward an interest-free loan.

2. Build a budget you can actually live with

A payoff plan only works if your monthly spending leaves room for it. Track your real income and expenses for a month, then decide how much you can direct to debt each month without running short. Our free Budgeting Template gives you a ready-made place to do this.

3. Pay the most expensive debt first

Make the minimum payment on everything, then put every extra dollar toward the debt with the highest interest rate. When it's gone, roll that payment into the next one. This approach, often called the debt avalanche, keeps the total interest you pay as low as possible. If you need quick wins to stay motivated, paying off the smallest balance first works too. The best method is the one you'll stick with.

4. Use lump sums strategically

Students often earn in bursts: summer jobs, co-op terms, tax refunds, and gifts. Decide in advance that a set share of each lump sum goes straight to debt. Automating a transfer on payday makes this much easier than relying on willpower.

Example

A student with a $6,000 line of credit at 8% puts $1,500 from each of two co-op terms and $150 a month from a part-time job toward it. The balance is gone in about two years, well before graduation, with much less interest than paying the minimum.

5. Keep a small emergency fund

Even a few hundred dollars set aside means a broken laptop or an unexpected trip home doesn't land on a credit card and undo your progress.

After graduation

Government student loans include a six-month non-repayment period after you finish school. If your income is low when repayment begins, the federal Repayment Assistance Plan can lower or pause your payments. Interest you pay on eligible government student loans may also qualify for a federal tax credit, which you can carry forward for up to five years.
