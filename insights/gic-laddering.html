GIC Laddering: Safety and Steady Income in Any Rate Environment

Guessing where interest rates are headed is hard. A GIC ladder means you don't have to, while keeping your money safe and some of it always coming due.

By Iberian Pacific Financial Services October 22, 2026 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • GICs guarantee your principal and a fixed rate for a set term.
  • A ladder splits your money across terms so part of it matures every year.
  • Deposit insurance limits apply per institution, so spread larger sums.
  • Hold GICs in a TFSA or RRSP to shelter the interest from tax.

Guaranteed Investment Certificates are one of the simplest ways to earn a predictable return. You lend money to a financial institution for a set term, and it guarantees your principal and a fixed rate of interest. The hard part is choosing the term: lock in for five years and rates might rise, or stay short and they might fall.

What a GIC ladder is

A ladder solves that problem by spreading your money across several terms instead of betting on one. A common version splits it evenly across one, two, three, four, and five-year GICs. Each year, one rung matures. You can use that money, or reinvest it in a new five-year GIC at the top of the ladder.

Example

You invest $50,000: $10,000 each in one, two, three, four, and five-year GICs. Next year, the one-year GIC matures. If you don't need the money, you reinvest it for five years. After a few years, every rung is earning a five-year rate, which is usually the highest, yet $10,000 still comes due every single year.

Why ladders work

  • No need to predict rates: if rates rise, maturing rungs are reinvested at the higher rate. If they fall, most of your money is still locked in at the older, higher rates.
  • Regular access to cash: part of your money becomes available every year without paying a penalty.
  • Predictable income: useful for retirees or anyone who needs a dependable amount on a schedule.

Keep deposit insurance in mind

Eligible GICs at Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation member institutions are protected up to $100,000 per insured category, per institution. Categories include deposits held in your own name, joint accounts, TFSAs, RRSPs, RRIFs, FHSAs, RESPs, and RDSPs. Credit union deposits are protected by provincial programs instead; in British Columbia, they're fully guaranteed. For larger amounts, spreading your rungs across several institutions keeps every dollar protected.

Mind the tax

Interest from GICs held in a non-registered account is taxed at your full marginal rate, and on longer compounding GICs, you must report the interest each year as it builds, even though you won't receive it until maturity. Holding your ladder inside a TFSA, RRSP, or FHSA shelters that interest.

Cashable or not?

Non-redeemable GICs usually pay more but lock your money in until maturity. Cashable or redeemable GICs let you withdraw early in exchange for a lower rate. A ladder reduces the need for cashable GICs, because some money is always coming due.
